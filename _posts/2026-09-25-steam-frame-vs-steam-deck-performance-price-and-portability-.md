Valve now has two portable gaming devices: the Steam Deck (handheld PC) and the Steam Frame (standalone VR headset). Both run SteamOS and share the Steam library, but they serve entirely different use cases. Here's how they compare on hardware, price, and portability.

---

## Specs at a Glance

| | Steam Frame | Steam Deck OLED |
|---|---|---|
| Chipset | Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 (ARM64) | AMD APU — Zen 2 CPU, RDNA 2 GPU |
| RAM | 16GB LPDDR5X | 16GB LPDDR5 |
| Storage | 256GB or 1TB UFS + microSD | 512GB or 1TB NVMe + microSD |
| Display | Dual 2160×2160 LCD per eye, 72–144Hz | 7.4-inch OLED, 1280×800, 90Hz |
| OS | SteamOS (Arch Linux) | SteamOS 3 (Arch Linux) |
| Wi-Fi | Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 5.4 | Wi-Fi 6E, Bluetooth 5.3 |
| Battery | 21.6Wh | 50Whr |
| Weight | 440g (with headstrap) | 640g |
| Launch | September 14, 2026 | May 2024 (OLED) |

---

## Price

**Steam Frame**: $1,059 (256GB) / $1,299 (1TB). Power supply sold separately at $29 — any 45W USB-C charger works. Box includes Steam Frame Controllers, Wi-Fi 6E adapter, and Half-Life: Alyx license.

**Steam Deck OLED**: $789 (512GB) / $949 (1TB) — prices increased in May 2026 due to component costs. Includes carrying case.

---

## Performance

The Steam Frame's Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 is optimized for VR workloads: driving two 2160×2160 displays at up to 144Hz while running inside-out tracking. For standalone VR and PC VR streaming, this is the right tool. It runs Android apps via Lepton compatibility and SteamVR titles via Wi-Fi 6E streaming from a PC.

The Steam Deck's AMD APU (Zen 2 + RDNA 2) runs traditional PC games natively. At 1280×800 resolution, it delivers playable performance on most Steam titles including recent AAA releases at medium settings. It cannot run VR content — the form factor and hardware aren't built for it.

---

## Portability

The Steam Frame is a VR headset: you wear it. The 440g total weight (185g core module plus headstrap and facial interface) is in line with other standalone VR headsets. Battery life is shorter than the Steam Deck at 21.6Wh — external battery packs clipped to a belt are a common solution for extended sessions.

The Steam Deck is a handheld console: you hold it. At 640g with integrated controls and a 50Whr battery lasting 3–12 hours depending on game load, it's suited for travel, commuting, and couch gaming. The included case makes it easy to carry.

---

## Ecosystem

Both devices run Steam. The Steam Frame's "Great on Frame" badge marks titles optimized for standalone VR play — Half-Life: Alyx is included and runs natively on ARM. The Steam Deck's Proton compatibility layer handles most Windows Steam titles.

The Steam Frame's primary advantage over Meta Quest 3 (its direct competitor) is Valve's data handling: Valve does not sell personal data, while Meta's VR data collection — including environmental mapping and eye tracking — has been a consistent user concern.

---

## Who Should Buy Which

**Steam Frame** — You want standalone VR with access to the Steam library, prefer Valve's ecosystem over Meta's, and have a capable PC for streaming demanding SteamVR titles. Suitable for users upgrading from older VR headsets with existing Steam libraries.

**Steam Deck OLED** — You want to play your Steam library in handheld form, don't need VR, and want a proven device with a wide compatibility track record. The $789 entry point is more accessible.

The two devices don't overlap — there's no use case where one substitutes for the other.
