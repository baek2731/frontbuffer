# How to Use WhatsApp on Android Auto

Android Auto supports WhatsApp for hands-free messaging while driving. Incoming messages are read aloud, and replies go out via voice — no typing required. This guide covers setup, how messaging works in practice, and what to do when it stops working.

---

## Requirements

- Android 8.0 or higher for wired Android Auto
- Android 11.0 or higher for wireless Android Auto (Android 10.0 for Pixel and Samsung devices)
- WhatsApp notifications enabled on your phone
- Android Auto app updated to the latest version

---

## How WhatsApp Works on Android Auto

When a WhatsApp message arrives while Android Auto is active, a notification appears on the car's display. Tapping it triggers the message to be read aloud. After playback, Android Auto prompts: "Do you want to reply?" Say "Yes" and dictate your response — the system converts it to text and sends it as a WhatsApp message.

To send a new message without waiting for one to arrive, activate Google Assistant by saying "Hey Google" or tapping the microphone icon, then say: "Send a WhatsApp message to [Contact Name]." Dictate the message when prompted, and Android Auto will confirm before sending.

All interaction is voice-driven or notification-triggered. Android Auto does not allow typing on the car's display while driving.

---

## Setup Checklist

**On your phone:**
1. Open **Settings → Apps → WhatsApp → Notifications** — ensure notifications are enabled
2. Open **Settings → Apps → Android Auto → Permissions** — confirm all required permissions are granted (Location, Microphone, Phone, SMS, Contacts)
3. Update both WhatsApp and Android Auto via Google Play

**In the car:**
Connect your phone via USB cable or wirelessly. WhatsApp should appear automatically in Android Auto's messaging section once permissions and notifications are configured.

---

## Troubleshooting

**WhatsApp messages not appearing in Android Auto**

First check: notifications. Go to **Settings → Apps → Android Auto → Notifications** and ensure they're enabled. A common failure mode is the system prompting "go to your phone and turn on notifications" even after you've enabled them — clearing Android Auto's cache usually resolves this.

To clear cache: **Settings → Apps → Android Auto → Storage → Clear cache**

**Connection drops or Android Auto won't launch**

Reset network settings on your phone: **Settings → System → Reset options → Reset Wi-Fi, mobile & Bluetooth**. You'll need to re-pair Bluetooth devices afterward.

If the issue persists after a cache clear and network reset, a factory reset of the car's infotainment system has resolved the problem for some users — including at least one 2024 Honda Accord Sport L Hybrid owner. Check your vehicle's manual for the specific procedure, as this erases all paired devices and settings.

**Previously connected cars causing conflicts**

Old pairings can interfere with new connections. In Android Auto: **Settings → Previously connected cars** — remove any cars you no longer use. On the car's head unit, delete your phone from its Bluetooth paired device list as well.
