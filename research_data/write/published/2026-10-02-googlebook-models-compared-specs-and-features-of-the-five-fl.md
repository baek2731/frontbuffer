Google's first five Googlebooks start between $899 and $1,299, and the cheapest is not the only way they differ. The Acer is the only convertible. The XPS and HP run Snapdragon chips. The Lenovo has the only 15-inch screen. This comparison uses the figures on Google's own comparison page.

*Checked October 20, 2026 against Google's comparison page and launch posts (September 21, 2026) and TechRepublic (September 23, 2026). Prices are US starting prices and can change. Battery figures are Google's claims, not test results.*

## What all five share

According to Google's [comparison page](https://googlebook.google/shop/compare/), [launch posts](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/devices/googlebook/first-look-googlebook/) and [FAQ](https://googlebook.google/frequently-asked-questions/):

- 16GB of RAM and 512GB of storage in the base configuration, with some models going up to 32GB of RAM
- A Titan C security chip, backlit keyboard, haptic glass trackpad, and touchscreen
- NPUs rated at 45 to 49 TOPS (Dell and HP 45, Acer and Lenovo 47, ASUS 49)
- Googlebook OS with Android apps from Google Play, Chrome with extensions, and Gemini features such as Magic Pointer, Rambler and Create My Widget
- A Glowbar light strip
- 12 months of Google AI Pro, 3 months of YouTube Premium, Adobe Photoshop and more, plus a year of GeForce NOW
- Regular feature drops and updates for up to 10 years

## Intel vs Snapdragon

The Acer, ASUS and Lenovo use Intel Core Ultra 5 chips, and the XPS and HP use Qualcomm's Snapdragon X Elite. Google's comparison page gives the Snapdragon models the longest battery claims, 18 and 19 hours against 13 to 16 for the Intel models.

Those figures conflict with Google's launch post, which says the lineup delivers up to 14 hours of video playback and 16 hours of web browsing. The 18 and 19 hour claims appear only on the comparison page, so treat them as best-case browsing numbers. We found no Google guidance on whether Android apps perform differently on the two chip families. TechRepublic says real-world differences between Intel and Snapdragon are among the biggest unknowns until independent testing appears.

## Model notes

**Acer Googlebook 14, from $899.** The cheapest model and the only convertible, so it is the one to look at if you want tablet mode. Acer told The Verge, as relayed by [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-googlebook-models-compared/), that $899 is an initial launch price that could change as component costs change.

**XPS Googlebook, from $999.** The only model with a non-OLED display, a 13.4-inch 2.5K LCD. It ties the ASUS for the lowest weight at 2.2 lb and carries an 18-hour battery claim.

**Lenovo Googlebook 15, from $1,099.** The biggest screen, 15.3 inches, and the heaviest body at 2.84 lb, with the shortest battery claim at 13 hours. Google calls it the ultimate workhorse. It supports face unlock as well as a fingerprint reader.

**ASUS Googlebook 14, from $1,299.** A 14-inch 2.8K OLED at 2.2 lb. Next to the $899 Acer, you pay $400 more for about a third of a pound less weight and no convertible hinge.

**HP Googlebook 14, from $1,299.** The longest battery claim in the lineup, 19 hours, and a Snapdragon chip. It supports face unlock as well as a fingerprint reader, and weighs 2.74 lb.

## Takeaway

The price ladder is clear: Acer at $899, the XPS $100 higher for lower weight and a longer battery claim on a smaller LCD screen, the Lenovo $200 higher for the large OLED screen, and the ASUS and HP at $1,299. Decide what you are paying for before stepping up, and check current prices, because Acer has said its launch price may not last.
