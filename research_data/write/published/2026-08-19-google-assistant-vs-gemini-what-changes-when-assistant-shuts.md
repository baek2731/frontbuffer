Google began removing Google Assistant from Android phones on September 4, 2026, and by late September reports said it was gone for many users. According to Google's email, as [9to5Google reported](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/04/google-assistant-september-2026-shutdown/), once availability is removed you can no longer use Assistant or switch back to it on your phone, tablet or paired devices. Below is what changed, what is not affected, and what to re-test now that Gemini is the assistant on your phone.

*Checked October 21, 2026 against 9to5Google, Thurrott, How-To Geek and Android Police. We did not open Google's own help page, and nothing here was tested on a phone.*

---

## What happened

- **Early August:** Google emailed existing Assistant users that Assistant was being discontinued and that Gemini is now the assistant experience on Android, per 9to5Google.
- **September 4:** removal began. Google said the process may take a few weeks to reach everyone, so devices lose Assistant on different days.
- **Late September:** [Android Police reported](https://www.androidpolice.com/the-google-assistant-kill-off-has-arrived/) on September 29 that many users could no longer switch from Gemini back to Assistant, and said 9to5Google and Android Authority had confirmed it. Its writer checked a Galaxy Z Fold 8 and a Pixel 10 Pro and found the option gone on both.

Google had planned the change for 2025. [How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/google-assistant-shuts-down-on-android-in-september/) says it slipped to 2026 and that Gemini had struggled with simple commands such as direct device controls. [Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/mobile/340138/google-assistant-to-be-discontinued-on-android-and-wear-os-on-september-6) instead says the delay came as Google built separate Gemini experiences for different device types. The reports give different reasons, and we found no explanation from Google in them.

---

## What is covered, and what is not

Per 9to5Google, the removal applies to Android phones, tablets and paired devices, Wear OS watches, headphones, and vehicles running Android Auto projected from a phone.

Not covered:

- **Cars with Google built-in** keep Assistant beyond September 4, 2026.
- **Google TV devices, Google Home speakers and smart displays** were not part of the announcement. Google mentioned Gemini is coming to them, and Thurrott notes there is already a Gemini for Home assistant on connected speakers and a Gemini for Google TV.

---

## If your phone has not switched yet

How-To Geek lists the steps to adopt Gemini early: install the Gemini app from the Play Store, then choose **Digital assistants from Google** and **Gemini**. Menu names may differ by phone. After removal reaches your device, that choice goes away.

---

## What to re-test

None of the reports we checked lists which Assistant features Gemini lacks, so run your own commands once. A practical order:

1. **Routines and reminders.** Open each one you rely on and run it by voice.
2. **Smart home commands.** Try the lights, climate and media devices you actually use.
3. **Media control.** Test the music or podcast app you use most.
4. **Wear OS and headphones.** Both are covered by the removal, so try your usual watch and earbud commands.
5. **Android Auto.** Try navigation, calls and messages through the car display. Our guide to [Gemini in Android Auto](https://frontbuffer.net/tech/gemini-in-android-auto-what-it-does-and-how-to-turn-it-on/) covers what it does there.

---

## Assistant versus Gemini on reliability

How-To Geek wrote that Gemini was good with knowledge requests, summaries and conversations, but struggled with simple commands like direct device controls, and that Google said it was confident Gemini would be more useful than Assistant. We found no independent test comparing the two on speed or accuracy, so we do not rank them. If a simple command that worked before now fails, report it to Google and use the phone's touch controls for that task in the meantime.

---

## Who will notice the change most

People who use voice for timers, calls, smart home control and media are the most likely to notice differences, since those are the commands Assistant handled in a fixed way. People who mainly ask open-ended questions will see the least change. Either way, the practical step is the same: run your five or six most-used commands once and note which ones need different wording.
