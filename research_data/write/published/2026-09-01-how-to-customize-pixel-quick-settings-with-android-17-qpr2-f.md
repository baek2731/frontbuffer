Android 17 QPR2 Beta 3 added a Quick Settings layout editor, a native App lock and wider blur effects to Pixel phones. Beta 4 arrived two weeks later and reached Platform Stability. 9to5Google reports that the stable QPR2 release is due in December 2026.

*Checked October 20, 2026 against reports from 9to5Google, Android Authority, Android Central and TechCabal. This is beta software, so features may change, and nothing here was tested on a phone.*

---

## Who can install it

[Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-17-qpr2-beta-3-new-features-3699084/) says owners of a Pixel 6a or newer can join the Android Beta Program for QPR2 Beta 3. For Beta 4, [Android Authority reports](https://androidauthority.com/android-17-qpr2-beta-4-3704596) that the Pixel 6 and Pixel 6 Pro are not supported, because they will be outside Google's software window by the stable release, and that the newest hardware in the support list was the Pixel 10 series, with no QPR2 build yet for the Pixel 11. You can enroll a phone at the [Android Beta Program](https://www.google.com/android/beta).

---

## The Quick Settings layout editor

Beta 3's new editor lets you reorder the layout of the Quick Settings panel, according to Android Authority. [TechCabal's feature rundown](https://techcabal.com/2026/08/17/android-17-qpr2-features/) describes the route: open Quick Settings, tap the edit icon, and go to the new **Layout** tab. Our [general guide to Quick Settings tiles]({% post_url 2026-08-25-how-to-customize-android-quick-settings-tiles %}) covers adding, removing and resizing tiles on Android 13 and later.

---

## Native App lock

[9to5Google reports](https://9to5google.com/2026/08/14/android-17-qpr2-app-lock/) that Beta 3 introduces a native App lock on Pixel:

1. Long-press an app icon. **App lock** appears in the menu next to App info, Pause app, Widgets and Remove.
2. Choose it to lock the app.
3. Manage everything under **Settings, Security & privacy, App lock**.

A locked app needs Fingerprint Unlock or your PIN before it opens. 9to5Google lists three side effects: notification content from the app is hidden, its widgets and shortcuts are removed, and AI agents and services you have allowed to access the app can still reach its data.

---

## Blur effects

Android Authority says Beta 3 expands the blur effect to more of the interface, including lock screen shortcuts, the fingerprint button and notifications. We found no setting that adjusts blur intensity in the reports we checked.

---

## Other changes

Android Authority also lists new protections against call-forwarding scams, more granular theming controls, and fixes for crashes in Quick Settings and incorrect battery health warnings in Beta 3. [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/apps-software/android-os/android-17-qpr2-beta-4-is-shaping-up-to-be-googles-best-beta-yet) says Beta 4 reached Platform Stability, which locks the APIs and shifts the work to polish and bug fixes, and that QPR2 limits some USSD-based forwarding commands and asks for confirmation when you dial them manually.

---

## Should you install the beta?

Beta 4 is more settled than earlier builds, and Android Central quotes one Pixel 9a owner calling it the most stable QPR2 build so far. It is still a beta. An Italian outlet, [TuttoAndroid](https://www.tuttoandroid.net/news/2026/09/01/android-17-qpr2-beta-4-platform-stability-pixel-11-1170444/), reports that a Bluetooth routing problem for incoming calls remained in all four QPR2 betas. If you rely on your phone daily, waiting for the stable release in December is the lower-risk choice.

---

## Takeaway

If you want the layout editor and App lock now, enroll a supported Pixel in the beta and expect rough edges. If you do not, the stable release should bring the same QPR2 features in December.
